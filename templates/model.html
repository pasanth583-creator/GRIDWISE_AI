1. Problem Statement

Modern university campuses face escalating electricity costs driven by volatile time-of-use (TOU) energy tariffs and steep monthly peak demand charges. While rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) and battery energy storage systems (BESS) offer significant economic potential, day-ahead energy procurement decisions must be committed under severe uncertainty regarding next-day weather patterns, solar cloud intermittency, and campus academic demand spikes.

Deterministic procurement schedules that rely solely on expected average forecasts frequently incur substantial imbalance penalties, suboptimal battery cycling, and unexpected peak tariff ratchets.

2. Proposed Solution

GRIDWISE AI implements a rigorous Two-Stage Stochastic Programming framework with Conditional Value-at-Risk (Mean-CVaR).

  • First-Stage Decisions: Day-ahead baseline grid energy reservations and target terminal battery state-of-charge committed prior to realization of uncertainty.
  • Second-Stage Recourse: Real-time battery charge/discharge redispatch, solar curtailment (if required), and spot energy adjustments across scenarios.
  • Risk Governance: Direct integration of Rockafellar-Uryasev CVaR convex constraints hedging against extreme 95th percentile cost outcomes.

4. Stochastic Optimization Foundations

Unlike deterministic optimization where parameters are assumed known with certainty, stochastic optimization models uncertain parameters as random vectors defined over a probability space \( (\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P}) \).

min_{x \in X} c^T x + \mathbb{E}_{\omega \sim \mathbb{P}} \left[ Q(x, \omega) \right]

Here, \( x \) represents non-anticipative first-stage procurement decisions, while \( Q(x, \omega) \) is the optimal second-stage recourse cost function under realization \( \omega \).

5. Two-Stage Stochastic Programming

The day-ahead microgrid procurement problem maps naturally into a two-stage sequential decision process:

Stage 1: Commit x = [ P_{grid}^{DA}(1), ..., P_{grid}^{DA}(24) ] Stage 2: Realize ω = [ P_{pv}(t, ω), P_{load}(t, ω) ] Solve y(ω) = [ P_{ch}(t, ω), P_{dis}(t, ω), P_{grid}^{RT}(t, ω) ]

By explicitly penalizing recourse deviations in the objective, the model hedges against costly peak grid imports without over-constraining daily flexibility.

6. Monte Carlo & Latin Hypercube Sampling

Continuous joint probability distributions of solar irradiance and campus load are discretized using Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) with Cholesky decomposition to preserve historical covariance structure.

LHS stratifies the multi-dimensional probability distribution into \( N = 50 \) equiprobable intervals, achieving superior coverage of tail extremes with significantly lower variance than standard pseudo-random Monte Carlo.

7. Sample Average Approximation (SAA)

Under Sample Average Approximation, the true mathematical expectation is approximated by the finite sample average over the \( |\Omega| \) generated scenarios:

\mathbb{E}_{\omega} [ Q(x, \omega) ] \approx \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \pi_\omega \cdot Q(x, \omega), \quad \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \pi_\omega = 1

This renders the large-scale stochastic problem into a deterministic equivalent linear program (LP) that is efficiently solvable via modern interior-point methods.

8. Mean-CVaR Risk Model (Rockafellar & Uryasev)

To prevent ruinous cost overruns during simultaneous weather disruptions and grid tariff spikes, we incorporate Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) at confidence level \( \alpha = 0.95 \). Using the Rockafellar-Uryasev variational formulation:

CVaR_\alpha(Cost) = \min_{\zeta \in \mathbb{R}} \left\{ \zeta + \frac{1}{1 - \alpha} \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \pi_\omega \cdot z_\omega \right\} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad z_\omega \ge Cost(\omega) - \zeta, \quad z_\omega \ge 0 \quad \forall \omega \in \Omega

Here, \( \zeta \) is an auxiliary decision variable representing the Value-at-Risk (VaR), and \( z_\omega \) represents the positive shortfall excess beyond VaR. The entire formulation remains strictly linear and convex.

9. Complete Objective Function

Convex LP Minimization
\min \quad J = (1 - \beta) \cdot \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \pi_\omega \left[ \sum_{t=1}^{24} \left( c_{buy}(t) P_{import}(t, \omega) - c_{sell}(t) P_{export}(t, \omega) + \lambda_{deg} (P_{ch}(t, \omega) + P_{dis}(t, \omega)) \right) + c_{peak} P_{peak}(\omega) \right] + \beta \cdot \left[ \zeta + \frac{1}{1 - \alpha} \sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \pi_\omega \cdot z_\omega \right]

Parameter \( \beta \in [0, 1] \) balances expected monetary expenditure against tail risk exposure, allowing campus operators to select an optimal position on the Pareto efficient frontier.

A. Campus Electricity Demand Formulation

Modeled as a superposition of base load \( D_{base} \), academic working hours diurnal cycle \( D_{diurnal}(t) \), day-type scaling factor \( \alpha_{day} \) (1.0 for weekdays, 0.62 for weekends), seasonal factor \( S(m) \), and an autoregressive AR(1) temporal noise process:

D(t) = \max\left(D_{min}, \; [D_{base} + D_{diurnal}(t)] \cdot \alpha_{day} \cdot S(m) \cdot (1 + \epsilon_t)\right) \epsilon_t = \rho \cdot \epsilon_{t-1} + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2} \cdot \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2), \quad \rho = 0.65
B. Solar PV Clear-Sky & Intermittency Model

Solar generation utilizes solar zenith angle geometry \( \theta_z(d, h) \), Haurwitz clear-sky global horizontal irradiance \( GHI_{clear} \), cloud attenuation factor \( (1 - 0.75 \cdot C^{3.4}) \), and crystalline silicon temperature derating:

P_{pv}(t) = \min\left(P_{pv}^{max}, \; P_{pv}^{cap} \cdot \frac{GHI(t)}{1000} \cdot [1 + \gamma_{temp} \cdot (T_{cell} - 25)] \cdot \eta_{sys}\right) \text{Strict Physical Bound: } P_{pv}(t) = 0 \quad \forall t \notin [\text{Sunrise}, \text{Sunset}]
C. Time-of-Use (TOU) Tariff Structure
